Gold River returns

The Mordum emergency broadcast

A summary of what Gold River (금강선) said, not a transcript. Each stage gathers related remarks; its VOD timecode is where that topic starts, and every timecode link opens the VOD there.

↓ next   ↑ previous   T talking points   P presenter mode

Why he is on camera

  • He opens by apologising for the disappointment since Mordum Extreme.
  • He is not returning as director or CCO: director Jeon stays in post, and Gold River is here to handle communication. He is making a single-player package game and spoke up because watching from the sidelines was hard.
  • Director Jeon came to him this week: setting a direction has been hard, and each broadcast adds misunderstandings. They talked it over and Gold River offered to speak for the team.
  • He had planned two to three weeks of prep. Reading the community, he saw resignation more than anger. "My rock-bottom antenna went off": letting this week pass would cost more players.
  • He lost sleep over the materials the night before; "forgive the design".

Our talking points

  • Is a former director handling comms a fix or a symptom?
  • Resignation, not anger, brought him back early. Is that what you saw?

Mordum Extreme: what went wrong

  • Questions he received: why release Mordum like this, what "drawing a line" meant, and whether the devs knew about the class disparity.
  • Purpose: an Extreme release to fill the off-season between Act 3 and Kazeros Extreme.
  • Mordum is a fixed-pattern fight (scripted, repeatable boss patterns) where some classes deal damage far more easily. The devs knew about the disparity; it was no surprise.
  • The chart: the DPS gap between classes spiked at Act 3 (he cites "over 20%" and promises exact figures next time), then narrowed through Act 4, Kazeros Extreme, Serca and Belgardin. Belgardin is the closest to what the devs intended.

Our talking points

  • The devs knew about the disparity but released it. Worse than not knowing, or more honest?
  • Belgardin or Armoche: which is the model raid?

Mordum Extreme: what went wrong

  • A Mordum-only balance fix felt like a cheap patch, so they kept the difficulty the director had announced.
  • The director meant to "draw a line" at one level; it ended up higher. The disparity meant some classes could not clear at all. "Wrong from the first button."
  • Then the dilemma: a nerf hurts players who already cleared; leaving it defeats the purpose. The window for a quick fix closed before the late nerf.
  • Kazeros Extreme is next week's concern: it should be harder than Mordum, yet any tuning now draws criticism. "The first button was buttoned wrong."

Our talking points

  • A line drawn too high, then a late nerf: which mistake cost more?
  • If you were tuning Kazeros Extreme this week, where would you put it?

Mordum: the decisions

  • No extension. His call, "shamelessly"; he accepts blame. Extending then nerfing again is "toying with live ops".
  • Better to admit the failed start, learn, and get the next content right.
  • No further nerfs. Clear rate: 3.47% before the nerf, 33.09% last night, about ten times as high. Still not what the director wanted, but another nerf would "wound the first-wave players twice".
  • The current event continues as is.

Our talking points

  • Would you have taken an extension over another nerf?
  • 3.47% to 33.09%: is "ten times" a fix or a different raid?

Mordum: the wording, and next week

  • "Drawing a line" was the wrong wording. Everyone assumes the line falls behind them. The purpose and exact difficulty target should have been stated plainly.
  • Apology for the loss players felt over Mordum.
  • Kazeros Extreme: similar to or slightly harder than Mordum. Its structure should reduce disparity. First-wave veterans may clear it faster. He would have preferred it easier.

Our talking points

  • "Everyone assumes the line falls behind them." Did you?
  • Kazeros Extreme "slightly above Mordum": relief or dread?

Balance and Ark Grid

  • He says balance has been a problem for eight years and he shares responsibility; "we'll try harder" rings hollow.
  • Ark Grid made balancing harder. It has not delivered the promised build freedom.
  • Review whether balancing through Ark Grid works; set a clear direction this winter.

Our talking points

  • Did Ark Grid deliver the promised freedom for your class?
  • Is "a direction this winter" enough, or do you need a date?

Synergy, supports, a safety net

  • Synergy (the party buffs each class brings) needs sorting out. First target: crit overcap (과치적: crit rate pushed past 100%, so part of the crit synergy is wasted); it is already under review. "I won't make you wait long."
  • Supports (Bard, Paladin, Artist): pick a direction first. Either make raids depend less on supports or, if raids keep this design, make supports easier to play well (easier upkeep of the attack buff and brand). Support fatigue and dependence are high; an adjustment is likely.
  • If class balance cannot be fixed, consider a system-level safety net for the classes that fall behind. Explicitly not class-change tickets; something you adjust at the roster level.

Our talking points

  • A roster-level safety net for weak classes: clever, or an admission balance is unfixable?
  • Which one: lower support dependence or a higher support floor?

Raid direction

  • Chat sentiment he read back: "the raids themselves are fun", "fun right up to Bel" (Belgardin), "fatigue is too high".
  • "Honest opinion": maybe Mordum Extreme should not have been released at all, only Kazeros Extreme. Releasing it may itself have been the problem.
  • Raid design has improved: more DPS windows, far fewer frustrations.
  • The data showed many mid-tier players left at Act 2 (Narok and Brelshaza, both gates too hard).

Our talking points

  • Mordum Extreme: should it have been scrapped or made easier?
  • Did you see mid-tier players in your friends list leave at Act 2?

Raid direction

  • He still wants challenge raids like Mordum and Armoche. But a raid like that should ship with Ark Grid builds that actually work in it; this time the raid and the systems were built separately and did not fit together.
  • Rehashed content is a problem, he says, and winter will not be a rehash. A lot is in the works.
  • Legion-style raids continue: more DPS time, fewer clone mechanics (the boss copying itself), experiments with fixed-pattern and scrolling-stage fights, frustrations removed.

Our talking points

  • Challenge raids plus prepared setups: would that have saved Mordum?
  • "Winter will not be a rehash." What would count as one for you?

The communication plan

  • An official broadcast, "Is Lost Ark OK as it is?" (로아 이대로 괜찮은가, 로이겐), with Gold River as MC asking questions. Director Jeon, team lead Kim Sang-bok (김상복) and a third lead (병탁, name unclear in audio) explain what is being built and why.
  • A "communication parade" from today through LOA ON Winter, not one big reveal. Without it, players would wait until the 8th anniversary broadcast on November 7.
  • Timing: he asked for about two weeks of prep, so roughly October 30.
  • Next time, the developers answer directly, "without evasive manoeuvres".

Our talking points

  • Is a two-week prep window enough for the broadcast?
  • What one question do you want the devs to answer live?

Speaking for the director

  • The director fears speaking live: phrases get misread ("the line", "Aldebaran is side content"). Gold River speaks for him until then.
  • He is not using a "chance"; he shares responsibility and came ready for criticism.
  • No hiding behind winter. Live service needs changes as it runs.
  • Leadership may change. They are not looking for a new director, but part of the team may get new leadership.

Our talking points

  • He fears speaking live. What would make you trust him on camera again?
  • Would new leadership fix the direction or just reset the clock?

Same-class rosters

  • In his era, the same-class-roster share was around 1%: people who genuinely wanted several of the same class. Summer 2024 (Season 3 start): about 6%, triple. Now: 17.8%.
  • He should have acted when it climbed 2.1 → 6 → 10% (his words; his chart shows 16.0% by summer 2025); that was the window.
  • Root cause: gem cost (skill gems, not Ark Grid Astrogems). Season 3's new systems demanded gold, early prices were high, and the burden never eased.

Our talking points

  • 17.8% same-class rosters: a player problem or a gem-price problem?
  • The window to act was 2024. What should have happened then?

Same-class rosters: what changes

  • An RPG that pushes you to play the same character many times is odd. They do not want to encourage it.
  • The company never stated a position, so punishing 17.8% now would be unfair. Winter: a clear stance, then changes.
  • No hard reset or "eradication". Flexible changes, mainly to gems, protect value and stop rewarding same-class rosters from winter on.

Our talking points

  • "Stop rewarding it going forward" vs "roll it back": which would you accept?
  • If you run a same-class roster, what would make you diversify?

The economy

  • He sees the gold problem as simple. Gold value is falling in sharp steps, not gradually.
  • Root cause: the pace of content releases. Each new raid has to pay more than the last (100 → 150 or 200). With raids every three months, gold supply grows fast. Sinks lag: adding them feels daunting when the game already feels expensive.
  • The economy breaks one beat later: when more gold becomes easy to farm at a low item level.

Our talking points

  • "One beat later": which raid's gold broke the economy, by his logic?
  • Faster raids or a stable economy: can you have both?

The economy

  • Plan: raids roughly every six months (Kazeros line, the next Shadow Raid, another raid type), finer reward scaling, horizontal non-raid content in between.
  • Raid and non-raid players used to be about half and half; mid-tier players have left over the pace, difficulty and cost.
  • Accumulated gold has to be drained, but cutting rewards on newly released content feels daunting too.
  • He argues packages matter less than people think: package-sourced gold peaked at 2.65% of all gold produced.

Our talking points

  • Does a six-month raid cadence fix the economy or just slow the bleed?
  • What would make you believe 2.65% came from packages?

Sea of Aldebaran and Rasamoa

  • The delay was not because it is "side content". The director meant endgame versus non-endgame; it came across badly. He apologises to story players.
  • Real reason: expanded scope. Rasamoa is the Sea of Aldebaran's only major city and, in the lore, the last and largest. Fully furnished interiors, visual upgrades, Sergaton content, more islands than a summer release could have had.
  • Screenshots shown, exterior and interior. "No AI was used."

Our talking points

  • Would you rather have had a smaller Aldebaran in summer?
  • "No AI was used." Why did he feel he had to say it?

Six-character limit, new class, engine

  • The six-character gold limit made sense for the economy and the mid-tier gap, but now holds back players raising new classes. Winter: a definite adjustment, not simply removing it; tied to economy checks and new-character growth incentives.
  • New class: December, on schedule, "one many of you asked for".
  • He suggests rethinking the summer/winter new-class schedule: fix what is broken first.
  • Engine and QoL in winter: deferred lighting, DLSS, party-finder work. The team says too little before "ta-da".

Our talking points

  • Should the six-character limit be lifted, raised or replaced?
  • Would you skip a new class for a season to get the fixes?

Rapid-fire from chat

  • The QA team is sizeable. Testing this time was clearly insufficient.
  • Headcount: OBT on 2018-11-07. By his exit (around June 2021), the team had grown by about 15; by December 2025, by 57. This year: three or four fewer, flat. Investment is not withheld; direction is the issue, not staffing. Staff work long hours.
  • Server transfer: he could not confirm; answers next time. He noted requests for guild content.
  • The "in-game is a party" meme: devs cannot laugh at it; they must make it true.
  • MMO traffic should be a wave, not a cliff; a wave needs off-ramps and on-ramps.

Our talking points

  • +57 people: do you believe "direction, not headcount"?
  • "A wave, not a cliff." Is a planned off-season healthy or just marketing?

Closing

  • All dev leads next time. Broadcasts continue through LOA ON Winter, good news or bad; "not just when it's good".
  • Kazeros Extreme is the next worry.
  • He ends with the Rasamoa BGM; he had little else he had authority to share.

Our talking points

  • What would make the follow-up broadcast in about two weeks a success for you?
  • Is Gold River handling comms a one-off or the new normal?
